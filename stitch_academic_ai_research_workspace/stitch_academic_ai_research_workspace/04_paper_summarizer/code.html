19 pages · 3.8 MB · Authors: Patrick Lewis, Ethan Perez, et al. (NeurIPS 2020)

Abstract Overview

This paper introduces Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), a hybrid architecture that combines pre-trained parametric sequence-to-sequence language models (BART) with a non-parametric dense vector index of Wikipedia accessed via Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR).

Research Objectives

Enable general-purpose language models to access, update, and cite external factual knowledge without full model retraining.

Problem Statement

Purely parametric LLMs store knowledge implicitly in weights, making provenance opaque, factual updates expensive, and hallucinations frequent.

Methodology

A bi-encoder retriever (DPR) encodes queries and 21M Wikipedia passages into MIPS-indexed vectors. The top-K retrieved documents are concatenated with the input and fed into a BART-large generator, trained end-to-end by marginalizing over latent documents at either the sequence level (RAG-Sequence) or token level (RAG-Token).

Key Findings

  • Sets state-of-the-art accuracy across Natural Questions (44.5 EM), WebQuestions, and CuratedTrec, outperforming parametric-only T5-11B.
  • Generates more specific, diverse, and factually grounded text than BART on Jeopardy question generation and MS-MARCO.
  • Swapping the non-parametric Wikipedia index updates world knowledge immediately with zero weight updates.

Results and Discussion

Human evaluators preferred RAG outputs over BART in 42.7% of factuality comparisons (vs. 7.1% for BART). RAG-Sequence excels at single-fact extraction, whereas RAG-Token synthesizes multi-passage answers more effectively.

Limitations

Retrieval latency scales with index size, and the generator can still ignore retrieved context when parametric priors are strong.

Conclusion

Hybrid parametric and non-parametric memory establishes a practical foundation for verifiable, updatable knowledge-intensive NLP systems.

Important Keywords

RAG Dense Passage Retrieval Non-Parametric Memory Open-Domain QA BART-large

Key Insight Highlights

Zero-Retraining Updates

Replacing the 2016 Wikipedia index with 2018 Wikipedia raised temporal QA accuracy from 12% to 68% without retraining.

Factuality Gain

42.7% human preference for factual accuracy over pure-parametric BART baseline.
